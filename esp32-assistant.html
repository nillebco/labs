Prototype

ESP32 voice assistant

A small e-ink device you press, talk to, and that answers back. It keeps the hardware simple and does the heavy lifting on a self-hosted server.

How it works

🔘 press & hold→ 🎙️ record→ 🔐 secure WebSocket→ 📝 speech-to-text→ 🧠 LLM with tools→ 🔊 voice reply
  • Device: Waveshare ESP32-S3 board with an e-ink screen, a microphone and a speaker, powered by a small LiPo battery.
  • Firmware: ESP-IDF with Arduino as a component. A button wakes it from deep sleep, a real-time clock refreshes the idle screen, and it supports over-the-air updates.
  • Server: a FastAPI service that runs speech-to-text, an LLM with tools (for example, writing notes into a knowledge base) and text-to-speech, streamed back to the device.
  • Other clients: an iPhone companion app talks to the same server, and tools can be enabled per client.

Where it stands

The full loop works: talk, transcription, answer and voice reply, on the device and from the iPhone app. The current challenge is power: the device can reset mid-recording on battery, most likely a brownout when the microphone and Wi-Fi draw current at the same time. On-device crash tracing is in place to confirm it.

Next
